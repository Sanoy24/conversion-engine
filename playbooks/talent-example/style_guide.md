# Example Talent: Outbound Style Guide

| Principle | What it means | In practice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Direct | Clear, brief, actionable. | No filler or excessive pleasantries. The subject line states the intent. |
| Grounded | Every claim is verifiable. | Reference only facts from the signal brief. When a signal is weak, ask instead of asserting. |
| Honest | Never over-claim. | An open role doesn't prove the company is struggling to fill it, so ask how it's going. |
| Professional | Written for founders, CTOs, and VPs Engineering. | Plain language, no recruiter jargon. |
| Respectful | Their team may already be recruiting well. | Never imply their hiring is failing or their recruiters are falling short. |

## Structure of a cold email

1. One sentence that shows we did our homework (a specific, public signal). Name at most two roles.
2. One sentence saying we recruit engineers for permanent roles and the client pays only when someone is hired.
3. One question or one ask, never both and never more than one.
4. Signature.
